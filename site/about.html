about the person who did this

Nalini. Thiruvananthapuram, mostly. I write documentation for a living, which means I spend my days making other people's software sound less frightening, and my evenings making one small corner of the internet look like 2002 on purpose.

I have been online since a dial-up connection that my family unplugged whenever the phone rang. I made my first page on a borrowed computer with a background of green pixels and a guestbook I refreshed forty times a day. ▣MANIFESTO.TXT — DRAFT 7

why a website, in 2026

Because a profile is something you fill in and a website is something you make. Because I want a place where the order of things is chosen by a person and not by a model guessing at my dopamine. Because I am tired of writing into a machine that answers back in my own voice.

This site is deliberately small. There is no framework, no bundler, no cookie banner, no consent theatre, no fonts loaded from a company that logs the request. It loads on a bad connection because it was built on a bad connection. It will still work when the services that host the rest of the web have pivoted twice and renamed themselves.

And it is not a product. There is nothing to convert here. No funnel, no email list, no “join the community”. If you like it, the transaction is: you make something too, and you tell me about it.

✦ ✦ ✦
1. hand-made over generated

Every word here was written by a person who was awake, in a room, with a fan on. The images were drawn or photographed by a person. If a machine helped, I would say so, and I am saying: it did not.

2. small over infinite

Seven field notes. Four shrines. One guestbook. A site can end. An ending is a feature: it tells you that you have seen everything and that you can go outside now.

3. links over feeds

A link is a recommendation made by a human, with their name attached, that they are willing to defend. There are thirty below. I have visited all of them. None of them were placed there by an algorithm.

4. no trackers, no ads, no data deals

No analytics script, no pixels, no third-party embeds, no newsletter. Fonts are served from this domain. Images are mine. The only thing stored is the visitor number and your guestbook entry, both in your own browser's local storage, which never leaves your machine.

5. readable, accessible, boring on purpose

Real contrast ratios, focus outlines that are not removed, alt text on every image, a skip link, semantic headings, and a “reduce motion” switch that actually reduces the motion. The Y2K look is the costume; the bones are modern HTML.

6. the frog is not a mascot strategy

The frog is a frog. She came from the bathroom, she stayed for the rain. Nothing is being sold.

▣LOCAL_WEB.KERALA

a note on the local web

The internet I grew up on was not global-first, it was nearby-first: cyber cafés, a college server, somebody's cousin's page about their dog. There is a version of the web where the most interesting site you visit in a day is made by a person who lives within cycling distance.

I would like that back. If you are in Kerala — or anywhere with rain and a bad router — and you have a page, sign the guestbook with your URL. I will link you from here. That is the whole growth strategy. It is also the only one I can afford.

next local meetup: a table at a café, laptops, no slides, first Sunday of the month. ask in the book.

COLOPHON / LICENSE

how this was built

One index.html, four sibling pages, one style.css, one main.js. No framework, no npm install, no build step — what is on the server is what I wrote. Uploaded with the Neocities CLI, which prints a small frog in the terminal, which is honestly a good enough reason to use it.

Typefaces: Press Start 2P, VT323 and Courier Prime, all SIL Open Font License, all served from this domain. The 88×31 buttons were made in a shell script, which is the most 2001 thing about them. The banner and photos were made by me.
